Nitheesh Research Tsunamis Risks and Facts: Health: Tsunamis can cause immediate death or severe trauma from the force of the waves and impact of debris. In the aftermath, floodwaters contaminated with sewage, chemicals, and other pathogens pose risks of infection, and long-term mental health issues like PTSD are common among survivors. Economic: Economic impacts are devastating, with billions of dollars in damage from property destruction, port disruptions, and lost industry revenue. The costs of reconstruction and recovery can put a severe strain on a nation's economy for years. Infrastructure: The powerful waves can destroy buildings, bridges, and roads and wash away entire towns. Widespread power outages and damage to water, gas, and communication lines can cripple essential services. Environmental: Environmental damage includes saltwater contamination of freshwater aquifers, rivers, and agricultural land, as well as soil erosion. The waves can also destroy sensitive coastal habitats like coral reefs and mangrove forests.  Nonprofit: American Red Cross: This organization responds to natural disasters, including tsunamis, by providing emergency assistance, food, and shelter to those affected. Tsunami Preparedness | Tsunami Safety Tips | Red Cross
Hailstorms Risks and Facts: Health: Large hailstones can cause concussions, fractures, and even fatal head injuries to people caught outdoors. Injuries can also occur from broken glass or falling debris during a severe storm. Economic: Hailstorms cause billions of dollars in insured losses annually, primarily from damage to property and vehicles. Agricultural losses can be catastrophic, as hail can destroy a year's worth of crops in a single storm. Infrastructure: Damage from hailstorms mostly affects exposed infrastructure, including roofs, windows, solar panels, and aircraft. The storms can also lead to power outages by damaging electrical lines. Environmental: Environmentally, hailstorms can damage or kill wildlife and strip foliage from trees and plants. The resulting destruction can harm local ecosystems.  Nonprofit: Thunderstorm Safety | American Red Cross.
Avalanches/Landslides Risks and Facts: Health: For avalanches, the main health risks are suffocation, trauma, and hypothermia for those buried in the snow. Landslides can cause death or injury from rapidly moving mud and debris, as well as long-term mental health impacts for survivors. Economic: Both events cause significant economic damage from the destruction of property and infrastructure. Avalanches can also disrupt local economies reliant on tourism, while landslides can reduce real estate values. Infrastructure: Avalanches can block critical transportation routes like roads and railways and damage structures not built to withstand the impact forces. Landslides can bury and destroy homes, damage utility lines, and disrupt transportation links for extended periods. Environmental: The environmental impacts include the destruction of forests and vegetation, soil erosion, and changes to water systems. Avalanches can create new habitats while also causing animal mortality and altering nutrient levels in aquatic systems.  Nonprofit: American Avalanche Association: This organization, in partnership with the U.S. Forest Service, offers information and education to promote avalanche safety and provides support resources for those affected. American Red Cross: The Red Cross provides general disaster relief for people affected by a wide range of emergencies, including landslides.  Landslide Safety & Preparedness Tips | American Red Cross American Avalanche Association

Viswa Research Notes Flooding Description: A flood is an overflow of water that submerges land that is usually dry, often resulting from heavy rainfall, rapid snowmelt, or other events that increase water levels. Health Risks: • Flooding can lead to the contamination of drinking and usable water supplies, creating a significant risk of waterborne illness to those who aren’t caught directly inside the flood, or to those living during and directly after a flood. • Still water is a breeding ground for mosquitoes, leading to increased incidences of diseases like malaria and dengue fever in places that have them. • Floods can cause direct physical injuries and fatalities to those who are caught in them directly. The direct impact causes risk of falling debris or electrocution from drowned power lines too. Economic/Infrastructure Risks: • The impact from a flood can cause severe damage to infrastructure, such as the destruction of homes, businesses, and public buildings. Repairs can cost large amounts of money and can disrupt local economies and displace communities. • Flooding can destroy agriculture lands, such as farms. The destruction of crops and livestock can lead to food shortages and economic hardship for farmers and communities reliant on agriculture. • Communities may face long-term displacement due to severe flooding, leading to social and economic instability as people are forced to relocate. Environmental Risks: • Floods can alter natural habitats, and the disruption of nearby ecosystems can lead to a loss of biodiversity. Sediment and pollutants from farms carried by floodwater can harm aquatic life and degrade water quality. • Flooding can lead to significant soil erosion, which can degrade land quality and reduce agricultural productivity in the long term.
Earthquakes Earthquakes Definition An earthquake is the sudden shaking of the Earth's surface caused by the release of energy stored in the Earth's crust. This energy release typically occurs along faults—fractures in the Earth's crust where tectonic plates meet and move. Health Risks • Injuries and fatalities: Collapsing buildings, falling debris, and infrastructure failures can cause serious injuries or death. • Mental health impacts: Survivors may experience PTSD, anxiety, and depression due to trauma and loss. • Disruption of healthcare services: Hospitals may be damaged or overwhelmed, limiting access to emergency care. Economic/Infrastructure Risks • Damage to buildings and roads: Homes, schools, bridges, and highways can be destroyed or rendered unsafe. • Business interruptions: Earthquakes can halt production, disrupt supply chains, and lead to job losses. • High reconstruction costs: Rebuilding infrastructure and homes requires significant financial resources and time. Environmental Risks • Landslides and soil liquefaction: Earthquakes can destabilize slopes and cause the ground to behave like a liquid, damaging ecosystems. • Water contamination: Broken pipelines and sewage systems can pollute water sources. • Wildlife displacement: Habitat destruction and noise can force animals to flee or perish.
Hurricanes Hurricane Definition A hurricane is a powerful tropical storm system characterized by strong winds, heavy rainfall, and low atmospheric pressure. These storms form over warm ocean waters and can grow to span hundreds of miles. When sustained wind speeds reach 74 mph (119 km/h) or more, the storm is classified as a hurricane. Hurricanes are categorized on the Saffir-Simpson scale from Category 1 (least severe) to Category 5 (most severe), based on wind speed. They can cause widespread destruction through storm surges, flooding, and wind damage. Health Risks • Injuries and fatalities: Flying debris, collapsing structures, and drowning during floods pose serious threats to life. • Waterborne diseases: Floodwaters can carry bacteria and viruses, increasing the risk of infections like cholera and hepatitis A. • Mental health issues: Survivors may suffer from trauma, anxiety, and depression due to loss of homes, loved ones, or livelihoods. Economic and Infrastructure Risks • Property and infrastructure damage: Homes, power lines, roads, and public buildings can be destroyed or severely damaged. • Disruption of utilities and services: Power outages, water supply issues, and communication breakdowns are common. • High recovery costs: Insurance claims, rebuilding efforts, and emergency response can strain local and national economies. Environmental Risks • Coastal erosion: Strong waves and storm surges can wash away beaches and damage coastal ecosystems. • Contamination of ecosystems: Floodwaters can spread pollutants, sewage, and hazardous materials into natural habitats. • Destruction of wildlife habitats: High winds and flooding can destroy nesting areas, forests, and wetlands, displacing animals.

Health Risks: • A significant risk induced by tornadoes are physical injuries from flying debris, destroyed structures, and strong winds. • Other major health risks include respiratory issues from pollution, infections from any contaminated wounds, and mental health issues like PTSD, anxiety, and depression. Economic/Infrastructure Risks: • Tornadoes are easily capable of causing billions of dollars in property damage, city infrastructure demolishment, and disruption in local businesses. Environmental Risks: • Tornadoes can erode soil, ruining agriculture and cropland. • Tornadoes can destroy forests and cause a significant loss of vegetation and wildlife • Tornadoes destroy habitats, potentially displacing wildlife and causing serious issues in ecosystems. https://www.scemd.org/prepare/types-of-disasters/tornadoes/ Wildfires Definition: Fires that spread quickly through forests, grasslands, or other areas with dry vegetation. They are often caused by human actions or lightning. Health Risks: • Wildfires can cause burns, breathing problems from smoke, and eye irritation. • People with asthma or other lung issues are at higher risk. Mental health problems like stress and anxiety may also happen. Economic/Infrastructure Risks: • Wildfires can destroy homes, roads, power lines, and businesses. • Firefighting and rebuilding can cost millions of dollars. With some places being more frequently impacted. Environmental Risks: • Wildfires burn trees and plants, kill animals, and damage soil. • Wildfires can also pollute water and air. https://www.scemd.org/prepare/types-of-disasters/wildfires/
Blizzards Definition: A strong snowstorm with heavy snowfall, freezing temperatures, and strong winds. It can last for hours or even days. Health Risk: • Blizzards can cause frostbite, hypothermia, and accidents from slipping on ice. • People may also get sick from being stuck without heat or food. Economic/Infrastructure Risk: • Snow and ice can damage buildings, roads, and power lines. • Businesses may close, and travel can be dangerous or impossible. Environmental Risk: • Blizzards can harm plants and animals, especially if they are not used to cold weather. • Snowmelt poses a significant risk of flooding. https://www.scemd.org/prepare/types-of-disasters/winter-weather/
Order of How Commonly the Disasters Occur in Alpharetta, Georgia 1. Hailstorms • Georgia experiences frequent thunderstorms, and hail is common during severe storms, especially in spring and summer. 2. Tornadoes • While not as frequent as in the Midwest, Georgia is part of “Dixie Alley,” so tornadoes do occur, especially during severe weather outbreaks. 3. Flooding • Heavy rain and storms can cause localized flooding, particularly in low-lying areas and near rivers. 4. Hurricanes • Alpharetta is inland, so direct hurricane strikes are rare, but remnants of hurricanes often bring heavy rain and wind. 5. Cold/Heat Waves • Heat waves are common in summer; cold snaps happen occasionally in winter, though extreme cold is rare. 6. Drought • Periodic droughts occur, especially in hot summers, but they’re usually moderate compared to western states. 7. Wildfires • Large wildfires are uncommon, but small brush fires can happen during dry periods. 8. Blizzards • Snowfall is rare and usually light; blizzards are extremely unlikely. 9. Earthquakes • Georgia has very low seismic activity; minor tremors happen occasionally but are rare. 10. Avalanches/Landslides • No mountains in Alpharetta, so avalanches are impossible; landslides are extremely rare. 11. Tsunami • Impossible—Alpharetta is far inland. 12. Volcanic Eruptions • No volcanoes anywhere near Georgia, so this is impossible.
