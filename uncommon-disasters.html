Nitheesh Research Tsunamis Risks and Facts: Health: Tsunamis can cause immediate death or severe trauma from the force of the waves and impact of debris. In the aftermath, floodwaters contaminated with sewage, chemicals, and other pathogens pose risks of infection, and long-term mental health issues like PTSD are common among survivors. Economic: Economic impacts are devastating, with billions of dollars in damage from property destruction, port disruptions, and lost industry revenue. The costs of reconstruction and recovery can put a severe strain on a nation's economy for years. Infrastructure: The powerful waves can destroy buildings, bridges, and roads and wash away entire towns. Widespread power outages and damage to water, gas, and communication lines can cripple essential services. Environmental: Environmental damage includes saltwater contamination of freshwater aquifers, rivers, and agricultural land, as well as soil erosion. The waves can also destroy sensitive coastal habitats like coral reefs and mangrove forests.  Nonprofit: American Red Cross: This organization responds to natural disasters, including tsunamis, by providing emergency assistance, food, and shelter to those affected. Tsunami Preparedness | Tsunami Safety Tips | Red Cross
Hailstorms Risks and Facts: Health: Large hailstones can cause concussions, fractures, and even fatal head injuries to people caught outdoors. Injuries can also occur from broken glass or falling debris during a severe storm. Economic: Hailstorms cause billions of dollars in insured losses annually, primarily from damage to property and vehicles. Agricultural losses can be catastrophic, as hail can destroy a year's worth of crops in a single storm. Infrastructure: Damage from hailstorms mostly affects exposed infrastructure, including roofs, windows, solar panels, and aircraft. The storms can also lead to power outages by damaging electrical lines. Environmental: Environmentally, hailstorms can damage or kill wildlife and strip foliage from trees and plants. The resulting destruction can harm local ecosystems.  Nonprofit: Thunderstorm Safety | American Red Cross.
Avalanches/Landslides Risks and Facts: Health: For avalanches, the main health risks are suffocation, trauma, and hypothermia for those buried in the snow. Landslides can cause death or injury from rapidly moving mud and debris, as well as long-term mental health impacts for survivors. Economic: Both events cause significant economic damage from the destruction of property and infrastructure. Avalanches can also disrupt local economies reliant on tourism, while landslides can reduce real estate values. Infrastructure: Avalanches can block critical transportation routes like roads and railways and damage structures not built to withstand the impact forces. Landslides can bury and destroy homes, damage utility lines, and disrupt transportation links for extended periods. Environmental: The environmental impacts include the destruction of forests and vegetation, soil erosion, and changes to water systems. Avalanches can create new habitats while also causing animal mortality and altering nutrient levels in aquatic systems.  Nonprofit: American Avalanche Association: This organization, in partnership with the U.S. Forest Service, offers information and education to promote avalanche safety and provides support resources for those affected. American Red Cross: The Red Cross provides general disaster relief for people affected by a wide range of emergencies, including landslides.  Landslide Safety & Preparedness Tips | American Red Cross American Avalanche Association
Volcanic Eruptions – when a volcano erupts lava out of itself Health There can be several gases that are released from volcanic eruptions that can negatively impact health, such as CO2, SO2, HCl, etc. Inhaling volcanic gases can cause irritation of eyes/airways, vomiting, headaches, difficulty breathing, and eventually bronchitis and lung cancer and possibly death. Economic/Infrastructure Can cause a lot of short-term economic effects such as destruction of crops, livestock and businesses can lead to job losses and reduced economic activity. Additionally, it induces the cost of emergency response services. Long-term economic impacts include the cost of repairing damaged infrastructure and has impact on tourism and agriculture due to changed environment. Environmental risks There are also several environmental impacts of volcanic eruptions. Plumes of ash can spread out over the sky, blocking the sun and reducing visibility. It can also reflect solar radiation and absorb land radiation, cooling the earth and creating volcanic winters. Nonprofit Preparing for Volcanic Eruptions
Cold/Heat Waves – Periods of extreme cold or hot temperatures Health Heat waves can cause damage to the brain, heart, lungs, kidneys, and liver at extreme temperatures. Cold waves can lead to hypothermia and dysrhythmia. Economic/Infrastructure Heat waves reduce worker productivity and cause $100 billion of losses a year. Environmental risks A lot of lives are lost every year to cold/heat waves (69,000 in the U.S. between 1999-2024), significantly affecting the population. Nonprofit Heat and Cold Waves
Droughts – Period of time with little or no water available Health Water can get contaminated due to reduced rivers and stream flows, making it unsafe to drink. Plus, due to reduced water reduced crop yield occurs, limited food access. Economic/Infrastructure Drought could cost countries 6% of their GDP due to water being used in production. On top of that, water demand and food prices will rise, which could lead to conflict and mass migration. Environmental risks Can alter the ecological balance of wildlife systems and harm fish, wildlife, and plants. Nonprofit Preparing for Droughts
