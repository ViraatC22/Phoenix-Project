Introduction: The Importance of Preparedness

Natural disasters can strike at any time, often with little to no warning. Being prepared is not just about reacting to an event; it's about building resilience and ensuring the safety of yourself, your family, and your community. This guide provides essential steps to take before, during, and after various natural disasters, helping you navigate uncertainties with confidence.

Before a Disaster: Preparation is Key

  • Emergency Kit: Assemble a kit with water (one gallon per person per day for at least three days), non-perishable food, a battery-powered radio, flashlight, extra batteries, first-aid kit, whistle, dust mask, plastic sheeting, duct tape, moist towelettes, garbage bags, wrench or pliers, manual can opener, local maps, and a cell phone with chargers and a backup battery.
  • Family Communication Plan: Establish a clear plan for how your family will contact each other if separated. Identify an out-of-state contact and a designated meeting place.
  • Home Safety: Secure heavy furniture, appliances, and water heaters to walls. Install smoke alarms and carbon monoxide detectors. Know how to shut off utilities (gas, water, electricity).
  • Evacuation Routes: Plan and practice multiple evacuation routes from your home and community. Know where local shelters are located.
  • Stay Informed: Sign up for local emergency alerts and monitor weather forecasts regularly.

During a Disaster: Act Quickly and Safely

  • Tornado: Seek immediate shelter in a basement, storm cellar, or an interior room on the lowest floor. Stay away from windows.
  • Flood: Move to higher ground immediately. Do not walk or drive through floodwaters. Turn around, don't drown!
  • Severe Storm (Thunderstorm/High Winds): Stay indoors, away from windows. Unplug electronics. If outdoors, seek sturdy shelter.
  • Earthquake: Drop, cover, and hold on. Get under a sturdy table or desk. If outdoors, move to an open area away from buildings.
  • Listen to Authorities: Follow instructions from emergency services and local officials.

After a Disaster: Recovery and Rebuilding

  • Safety First: Check for injuries and administer first aid. Avoid damaged buildings and downed power lines.
  • Contact Family: Let your family know you are safe. Use text messages or social media if phone lines are jammed.
  • Assess Damage: Document damage with photos for insurance purposes. Report damage to authorities.
  • Stay Informed: Continue to monitor news and emergency broadcasts for updates and instructions.
  • Help Others: If you are able, assist neighbors or community members in need.
